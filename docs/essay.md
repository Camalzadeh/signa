# Project Essay: Signa – Bridging Signal Intelligence and AI

My name is Humbat Jamalov, and I am a Computer Science student at the French-Azerbaijani University (UFAZ). Currently, I work as a Software Developer, where I build full-stack applications using Flutter and Django. Although I have a strong command of Java, I had never explored Kotlin until this competition. This contest provided a unique opportunity for me to master Kotlin and Kotlin Multiplatform (KMP), allowing me to expand my technical horizon beyond my professional routine.

The inspiration for Signa was born during my "Signal Processing" course at university this year. I became deeply interested in the invisible signals that surround our devices and the potential security vulnerabilities they represent. I realized that malicious signals could interact with our hardware without our consent. My vision for Signa is to empower users to manually monitor these signals and eventually create a secure communication ecosystem where people can exchange data using custom protocols, making it far safer than standard networks.

Upon discovering the Kotlin Student Contest, I dedicated myself to transforming this idea into a functional cross-platform application. Signa performs real-time signal scanning every five seconds, ensuring that every detected frequency is immediately persisted into a local Room database so that no critical data is lost. In the signal details view, users can visualize the strength and frequency history of a specific signal. To enhance security, I integrated Gemini AI to provide automated audits. This allows users to understand the risks associated with a signal, with all AI reports stored locally for privacy and future reference.

I have ambitious plans for the future of Signa, including background analysis for immediate threat detection and implementing Fourier Transform algorithms for deeper spectral analysis. I also aim to enable secure messaging between devices using custom signal protocols. This project demonstrates how Kotlin Multiplatform can be used to build complex, security-oriented solutions that bridge the gap between academic theory and practical, real-world applications.
